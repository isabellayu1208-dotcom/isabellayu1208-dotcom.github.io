FairPlay Lab

I built this at SoftCom Lab, Cal Poly Pomona, with Kyler Harris

I built a mobile app that turns attendance, RPE, match minutes, and pitch events into substitution advice a volunteer coach can open and audit. I kept the scoring rule-based so every suggestion names the inputs that produced it, then I audited the engine and found that RPE moved minutes the most and that a high-load threshold inverted the intended penalty. The paper was accepted at EDUAIT 2026, the app is on the App Store, and I filed U.S. provisional patent 64/164,480. I beta-tested it on my varsity team.

Accepted · The Curieux Review

AI detectors on ELL school essays

I did this study, mentored by Dr. Mani at Carnegie Mellon

I tested whether free AI writing detectors flag English-learner school essays more often than native ones, the pattern reported on TOEFL text. I scored two samples of 200 human-written ASAP 2.0 / ELLIPSE essays with ZeroGPT and an open RoBERTa classifier. ELL essays were not flagged more often; false positives tracked shorter, less lexically diverse writing instead. The paper has been accepted by The Curieux Review; the practical consequence is that a school should not treat a single detector score as misconduct.

UChicago Summer Immersive · 2026

School choice lotteries

My team — Irene Gong, Kristen Gumagay, and me — presented Cullen, Jacob & Levitt (2006)

My team walked through Chicago school-choice lotteries to separate ITT from LATE: winning a seat is random, actually enrolling is not. Winners attended objectively better schools, but test scores and graduation barely moved, while arrests and discipline fell. That null academic result is the finding I now carry into my senior project, instead of treating it as a failed design.

In progress · Senior project

School resources, not school type

I am designing this as my Basis Independent Brooklyn senior research, 2026–27

I am asking whether NYC public–private gaps in academic and college outcomes are explained by measurable resources rather than the sector label. It follows the UChicago lottery paper: a better school name did not raise test scores, so the next question is which resources we can actually name. I will use public school-level files and observational comparisons; data collection has not started.

Should we be polite to ChatGPT?

I wrote this for the John Locke Institute Essay Competition, Science & Technology, 2026

I argued that politeness to chatbots is not empty courtesy: it can improve answers, keep civility in practice, and hedge against losing control. I used prompt-politeness studies and moral-psychology accounts of digital manners. The essay received a Commendation in Science & Technology.
